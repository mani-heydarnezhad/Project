Discrete mathematics is the branch of mathematics dealing with objects that can
assume only distinct, separated values. It includes graph theory, combinatorics,
number theory, and mathematical logic. Cryptography relies heavily on discrete
mathematics, especially number theory and combinatorics. Classical ciphers such
as the Caesar cipher, the substitution cipher, and the Vigenere cipher are simple
historical examples of how mathematical structures can be used to protect
information. Modern cryptography extends these ideas using finite fields, modular
arithmetic, and computational complexity theory to build systems that are secure
against powerful adversaries. The study of these classical methods also teaches
valuable lessons about statistical analysis, since natural language exhibits a
highly non uniform distribution of letters, and this property can be exploited
to break ciphers that do not sufficiently randomize the underlying text.
